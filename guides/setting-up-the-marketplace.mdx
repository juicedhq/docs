---
title: Setting Up the Marketplace
description: Configure reverse-auction pricing per lead type, add a Marketplace stage to your distribution, and set fallback prices for ping-post sources.
---

The marketplace is a reverse auction where unsold leads (and any lead your distribution pipeline decides to list) drop in price over time until a customer buys them. Setting it up takes three passes: auction pricing on each lead type, a Marketplace stage in your distribution, and fallback prices on any source that pings. This guide walks all three.

For the conceptual model — how the floor moves with buy orders, what customers see, the full auction lifecycle — see the [Marketplace feature page](/features/marketplace). For the customer-side buying experience, see the [Marketplace page](/customer-portal/marketplace) in the customer portal reference.

## Prerequisites

- Admin role access to your tenant.
- At least one [lead type](/features/lead-types) with active inventory.
- At least one [source](/features/sources) generating leads for that lead type.
- A pricing plan — what price should leads start at, where should they floor, and how fast should they drop?

## Step 1: Enable the marketplace per lead type

Each lead type opts in to the marketplace on its own, with its own auction curve.

Open **Lead Types** under **Ingest** in the sidebar, click a lead type, then click the **Marketplace** tab.

  <img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/tenant/ingest/lead-type-marketplace-dark.webp" alt="Lead type Marketplace tab with Enable marketplace on, First Price, Last Price, Increment, and Interval filled in, and a summary sentence describing the auction" />
</Frame>

Turn on **Enable marketplace**. Four pricing fields appear:

| Field | What it does |
|-------|--------------|
| **First Price** | Where the auction opens. Customers see this as the initial price the moment a lead enters the marketplace. |
| **Last Price** | Where the auction floors. Once the price drops to this, it stops dropping. If no customer has bought by then, the auction ends **Unsold**. |
| **Increment** | How much the price drops each tick. E.g., `5` means every interval the price drops &#36;5. |
| **Interval** | How long between ticks, in minutes. E.g., `120` means a price drop every two hours. |

As you type, a plain-English summary below the fields updates — "Your auction will start at &#36;75.00 and end at &#36;15.00. The price will drop by &#36;5.00 every 120 minutes. The auction will last for a total of 1560 minutes." That's the sanity check that your numbers feel right.

Click **Save changes** on the save bar. Leads of this lead type can now be listed by a Marketplace stage.

<Note>
Each lead type has its own auction curve. A fresh lead can start high with a steep decline, while an aged lead can start low and floor even lower — tune each independently.
</Note>

## Step 2: Add a Marketplace stage

A lead doesn't enter the marketplace just because its lead type allows it — it has to be *routed* there by a **Marketplace** stage in your [distribution stages](/features/distribution).

Open **Distribution Stages** in the sidebar. If you don't already have a Marketplace stage, click **New stage**, choose **Marketplace** as the **Method**, and give it a name. Add **Eligibility Conditions** if only some leads should be listed. Click **Create stage**.

Order matters: stages run top to bottom, so the usual spot for a Marketplace stage is after your bidding stage (leads nobody bid on get a second chance). If a lead's auction ends unsold, distribution resumes with the stages below it. See [Configuring distribution stages](/guides/configuring-distribution-stages) for ordering and the "fresh, then aged" pattern.

## Step 3: Set fallback prices on ping-post sources

If a source sends [pings](/features/ping-post) before posting, a Marketplace stage can only accept the ping when the source has a **fallback price** for the lead type. The fallback is the price Juiced quotes back in the ping response. Sources that only post leads directly can skip this step.

Open **Sources** under **Ingest**, pick a source, and click the **Pricing** tab. Each lead type in the source's category gets a row with a **Fallback Price** input. Enter the price you're willing to pay the source for a lead that ends up on the marketplace; it saves when you leave the field.

Got several sources on the same category? **Copy from Source** in the **More actions** menu copies another source's prices without overwriting any you've set. See [Source pricing](/features/sources#source-pricing).

## Step 4: Turn on the public marketplace (optional)

Want prospects to browse your inventory before signing up? Click the settings gear in the rail, choose **Domains & Links** under **Account**, and turn on **Enable public marketplace** in the **Public Marketplace** section.

Visitors can then browse live auctions at `/marketplace` on your domain without logging in. Buy buttons are replaced with **Sign up to purchase**, which sends them to registration. The same page lets you choose whether unauthenticated visitors who follow a customer link land on the public marketplace or the sign-in page.

<Tip>
Turn on the public marketplace when you want it to double as a live shop window for sales — it's usually easier to close prospects who can already see your inventory and pricing.
</Tip>

## Step 5: Verify with a test lead

The surest check is a real lead that no bidder will match.

1. From a source with test mode **off**, send a lead for a lead type with the marketplace enabled, in a location with no matching bids (see [Sending leads to Juiced](/guides/sending-leads-to-juiced)).
2. Open **Leads** and pick the **Marketplace** tab. The lead should be there.
3. Log in as a test customer with a funded wallet and open **Marketplace** in the customer portal. The lead should appear at the First Price, with a countdown to the next price drop.

<Note>
Test mode's zip `00005` returns a marketplace response without running your stages or starting a real auction. It's handy for an integrator checking response handling, but it won't prove your marketplace setup works.
</Note>

If a real lead doesn't land on the marketplace:

- Check the lead type's **Marketplace** tab has **Enable marketplace** on.
- Check you have a Marketplace stage, it isn't paused, and its eligibility conditions match the lead.
- For pinged leads, check the source's fallback price is set for that lead type.
- Open the lead's incoming log (**View Log** on the lead) and read the Marketplace stage's result in the timeline. See [Logs](/features/logs).

## Step 6: Read the marketplace activity (optional)

Once real leads are flowing:

- **Customers buy leads** via **Buy now** (instant) or **buy orders** (set a target price; the amount is withdrawn from their wallet right away and the sale fires when the auction price reaches their target). See [the customer marketplace page](/customer-portal/marketplace) for the full buyer walkthrough.
- **Sold leads** move from the **Marketplace** tab to **Assigned** on the Leads page.
- **Unsold leads** continue to the next distribution stage. Auctions are kept for 90 days, then pruned.
- **Wallet transactions** — Each buy is a regular lead charge. Each buy order appears as **Placed Buy Order** when the amount is withdrawn, then comes back as **Canceled Buy Order** (customer cancelled) or **Released Buy Order** (auction ended or the order won). See [Transactions](/features/transactions).

## What happens next

With the marketplace running, the natural follow-ups are:

- **Tune pricing** — Watch which lead types sell fast vs. floor unsold. Drop the First Price on the stale ones, raise the Last Price on the ones selling instantly.
- **Customize buy order notifications** — Customers with outbid buy orders want to know. The default template usually works; customize it under **Settings → Application → Notification Templates** if you want your own copy. See [Customizing notification templates](/guides/customizing-notification-templates).
- **Run the marketplace twice** — If you want a fresh pass and an aged pass, put an **Alter Data** stage between two Marketplace stages to switch the lead to an aged lead type. See [Configuring distribution stages](/guides/configuring-distribution-stages).
- **Promote the public marketplace** — Link your marketing site to `/marketplace` so prospects see live inventory. Pair it with your sign-up call to action.

The marketplace is the workhorse of lead sell-through — once tuned, it quietly squeezes value out of overflow without babysitting.
