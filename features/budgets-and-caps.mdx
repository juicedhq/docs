---
title: Budgets & Caps
description: Spending limits and lead volume limits that govern what customers can buy.
---

Budgets and caps are the governors that keep lead flow inside the boundaries a customer (or you) has agreed to. Caps limit **how many** leads can be bought; budgets limit **how much** can be spent. Both are evaluated continuously, and a bid is only eligible to win a lead when every applicable limit has headroom.

## Customer and bid levels

Limits exist at two scopes.

- **Customer-level** limits apply across all of a customer's bidding activity. They're useful for account-wide guardrails (e.g., "this customer can spend at most $10,000 per month").
- **Bid-level** limits apply only to a specific bid (one lead type in one location). They're useful for fine-grained control (e.g., "cap this auto-insurance bid at 50 leads per day").

A bid has to clear **both** its own limits and its customer's limits to win a lead. Whichever is most restrictive wins.

## Turning limits on

Every limit is off until you enable it. Open **Settings → Application → Bidding** and use the switches under **Lead Cap Settings** and **Budget Settings**. There are twelve in total, covering **Bid** and **Account** versions of daily, weekly, and monthly caps and budgets. Save with the floating save bar.

Each switch controls one field.

- **Account** switches add that field to the **Bidding Limits** section when you [create or edit a customer](/features/customers#creating-and-editing-a-customer), and to the customer's own [bidding limits](/customer-portal/bidding-limits) page in their portal. With no Account switches on, the section is hidden entirely.
- **Bid** switches add that field to a bid's **Bidding limits** section, and the matching **Caps** and **Budgets** columns to the customer's bid list. Bid-level limits only appear when editing an existing bid, not when creating one.

<Note>
Turning a switch off doesn't just hide the field. Limits of that kind stop being enforced, even if customers had values saved. Turn it back on and the saved values apply again.
</Note>

## Caps vs. budgets

| | Counts | Useful for |
|-|--------|------------|
| **Lead cap** | Number of leads assigned | Volume governance ("no more than 100 leads this month") |
| **Budget** | Dollars spent on assigned leads | Cost governance ("no more than $2,000 this month") |

Both dimensions come in three interval flavors (**daily**, **weekly**, and **monthly**). You can configure any combination, including all six at once.

## Evaluating limits at once

Every limit you configure is checked simultaneously on every auction. A lead only flows to a customer when all of these are true.

- The customer's daily, weekly, and monthly lead caps all have room for one more lead
- The customer's daily, weekly, and monthly budgets can all cover the bid amount
- The bid's daily, weekly, and monthly lead caps all have room
- The bid's daily, weekly, and monthly budgets can all cover the bid amount

The first limit to exhaust wins. If a bid has a daily cap of 10 and a daily budget of \$500 with bids at \$40 each, the cap trips first (10 leads × \$40 = \$400, under budget). If the bid was \$80, the budget would trip first (6 × \$80 = \$480, under cap; 7th lead would push past \$500).

## Reset intervals

- **Daily** limits reset at midnight UTC.
- **Weekly** limits reset at the start of the UTC week (Monday 00:00 UTC).
- **Monthly** limits reset at the start of the UTC month.

Resets are calendar-based, not rolling. A lead assigned at 23:59 UTC counts against the daily cap for one minute before the counter clears.

## What counts toward caps and budgets

Caps and budgets only count **leads assigned through bidding**. Leads a customer purchases directly from the [marketplace](/features/marketplace) don't count, which is why these are sometimes called "bidding limits." The marketplace is a separate purchase channel that draws from the same wallet but doesn't flow through the bidding system.

Returned leads stop counting. Once a lead is assigned it counts against caps and budgets, but if it's [returned](/features/returns#returning-leads), it no longer counts toward the customer's or bid's lead caps or budget spend, so the headroom comes back.

## How limits affect status

Limits roll up into the statuses that drive whether a bid or customer is competing.

### Bid status impact

| Underlying status | Triggered when | Displays as |
|-------------------|----------------|-------------|
| **Cap** is Exhausted | Any of the bid's or customer's lead caps has been reached | <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> |
| **Budget** is Limited | The remaining budget is below the full bid amount but above zero | <span className="tone-badge tone-sky">Limited</span> (bid still participates at the reduced amount) |
| **Budget** is Exhausted | Any of the bid's or customer's budgets has been fully consumed | <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> |

See [Bidding → Bid statuses](/features/bidding#bid-statuses) for the full display-status breakdown.

### Customer status impact

Caps and budgets **do not** change a customer's display status. The customer's display status reflects operational state and wallet funding only. When a customer hits a cap or budget, the effect shows up on each of their **bids**. Every bid flips to <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> and stops competing. The customer themselves can still show as <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Active</span>. They're just not receiving leads until the constraint resets.

If you're trying to diagnose why a customer isn't getting leads despite showing Active, check the bid list for <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> rows.

## Leaving a limit blank

Every limit is optional. Leaving a field blank means "no limit" for that dimension. A customer with only a monthly budget set will have unlimited daily and weekly spend (up to that monthly ceiling) and unlimited lead counts.

## Recovering from exhausted state

Exhausted bids and customers recover automatically.

- **Cap exhaustion** resolves when the interval resets.
- **Budget exhaustion** resolves when the interval resets.
- **Funding exhaustion** (separate from budgets, as covered in [Bidding → Bid statuses](/features/bidding#bid-statuses)) resolves when the customer's wallet is topped up.

No manual intervention is required to bring a bid back online once the underlying condition clears.
