---
title: Reading Location Analytics
description: Find geographic supply-and-demand gaps by comparing bids, customer interest, and lead delivery across countries, states, and counties.
---

Location analytics is the map of your marketplace. It tells you where customers *want* leads (active bids, top prices, interested buyers) and where they're actually *getting* them (received, assigned, and charged amounts). When the two sides don't match (heavy demand in Miami but barely any leads flowing), that's a supply gap you can close with more sourcing, better ad targeting, or price adjustments. This guide walks through using the analytics views to find those gaps and act on them.

For the metric definitions themselves, see the [Location analytics feature page](/features/location-analytics).

## Prerequisites

- A role that can view analytics. If the **Locations** group isn't in your sidebar, ask an admin on your account.
- At least a few weeks of lead and bid history. The metrics need volume to be meaningful, so fresh workspaces will see mostly zeros.
- (Optional) A specific question you're trying to answer: "Where are we underpriced?", "Which states have unmet demand?", "Do any counties have bids but no leads?"

## Step 1: Open the Locations views

In the sidebar, find the **Locations** group. It has three pages:

| View | What it lists | Best for |
|------|---------------|----------|
| **Counties** | Every county (or your country's equivalent) across your operating countries. | Hyper-local analysis: "where exactly is demand concentrated?" |
| **States** | Every state or territory. | Mid-level territory planning: "which states are underserved?" |
| **Countries** | Every country your workspace operates in. | Multi-country workspaces rolling up national demand. |

Single-country workspaces see that country's own terms automatically. Multi-country workspaces see combined labels such as **States / Territories** and **Counties / Postcodes**.

Click **Counties** (or whichever level fits your question). The table loads with one row per location.

## Step 2: Read the two sides of the table

### Bids: the demand side

| Column | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Active** | Count of fully operating bids set at this level: customers who are *ready to buy right now*. Paused, capped-out, or out-of-budget bids aren't counted. On States and Countries, only state-wide and nationwide bids count. |
| **Top Bid** | Highest current bid amount in the location. Tells you the ceiling someone is willing to pay. |

### Leads: the supply side

| Column | What it tells you |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Received** | On Counties, every lead that came in for the county during the timeframe. On States and Countries, only leads assigned at state-wide or nationwide scope. |
| **Min Value** | Lowest charged amount among those leads. |
| **Max Value** | Highest charged amount among those leads. |
| **Avg Value** | Average charged amount among those leads. |

The gap between **Active** and **Received** is the headline signal. Many active bids but few received leads means unmet demand, which usually means ad spend or source partnerships belong here. Few bids but many leads means oversupply, which usually means you should recruit more buyers or lower prices.

<Tip>
Sort by **Active** descending to find your hottest territories. Then scan **Received** next to each: if it drops off sharply, you've found a supply gap.
</Tip>

## Step 3: Pick a timeframe and filter by lead type

The lead columns cover the timeframe in the top-right of the page: **Billing period**, **Today**, **7D**, **4W**, **3M**, **12M**, **MTD**, **Last month**, **YTD**, **All**, or a **Custom** range. The bid columns always reflect your bids as they are today.

Then click **Filters** and use **Lead types** to narrow the view to one or more lead types. Every column recomputes against just that slice.

This is where the analysis gets actionable:

- **Unfiltered view** gives you the aggregate picture, useful for territory strategy.
- **Filtered to one lead type** surfaces product-specific gaps: "we have 40 bids on this lead type in Texas but only 12 leads landed last month."

The Counties view adds a **State** filter, plus **Country** for multi-country workspaces, so you can narrow to a region before exploring counties. Filters live in the page URL, so you can bookmark a view you check often.

Click any row to open the location's page.

### Overview tiles

- **Active Bids** — Bids ready to receive leads here.
- **Interested Customers** — Distinct customers with *any* bid on this location, including paused or underfunded ones. The gap between Active Bids and Interested Customers is latent demand: customers who've expressed interest but aren't currently buying. When you re-activate them (fund wallets, lift caps), that demand becomes real.
- **Top Bid** — Highest current bid amount.

### Values

Four more tiles for the selected timeframe (the last 30 days unless you've picked another), each compared with the previous period of the same length:

- **Leads assigned**, with a sparkline.
- **Minimum / Maximum / Average Value**.

Rising values mean the market is heating up here; falling values mean it's cooling. Combine with the Active Bids count: rising values plus plenty of bids means double down; falling values plus few bids means reconsider your investment.

### Bids

A table of the bids set at exactly this level (county bids on a county, state-wide bids on a state, nationwide bids on a country), with the customer, customer status, lead type, amount, and the bid's **Operational**, **Funding**, **Cap**, and **Budget** status. Sort by amount to see who's actually at the top. Use **Filters** to find, say, every bid here that's out of funds, then follow up with those customers from their customer page.

### Child locations

The sidebar lists what's inside the location as plain text: a county shows its postal codes, a state shows its counties. Country pages don't list children; go to **States** and filter instead.

## Step 5: Export counties to CSV (optional)

The Counties page has an **Export** button in the top-right. The other views don't: counties are the most granular level, so that's where exports are most useful.

Click **Export**. Your lead type and location filters are respected, but the timeframe isn't. The file is built in the background, and a **County export complete** notification lands in the [notification bell](/features/dashboard#notifications) with a download link when it's ready.

Use the CSV for:

- Ad-spend allocation models: pull it into a spreadsheet, overlay cost data, and compute cost per assigned lead by county.
- Source-partnership pitches: show a partner the specific counties where you have demand but no supply.
- Territory reviews in quarterly business reviews.

<Note>
States and Countries don't have an export. If you need a state-level roll-up, export counties and pivot by state in your spreadsheet.
</Note>

## Step 6: Act on the patterns you find

The analytics views are only useful if they change what you do. A few common patterns and the actions they imply:

**Pattern: Many active bids, few leads received**
- High demand, low supply — close the gap.
- Actions: recruit new sources for that region, increase ad spend on geo-targeted campaigns, or onboard a partner with supply there ([Adding a partner](/guides/adding-a-partner)).

**Pattern: Few active bids, many leads received**
- Oversupply — not enough buyers, or prices are wrong.
- Actions: recruit more customers in that region, run a marketing campaign targeting those customers, or revisit pricing (see [Bidding](/features/bidding)).

**Pattern: High Top Bid, low Avg Value**
- Customers are willing to pay a lot, but auctions are clearing low — likely thin competition.
- Actions: tell existing customers about the territory (notifications, banners — see [Publishing a banner](/guides/publishing-a-banner)), or raise the minimum bid on the lead type's bidding scopes (see [Setting up bidding](/guides/setting-up-bidding)).

**Pattern: High Interested Customers, low Active Bids**
- Lots of paused or underfunded bids. The demand is latent.
- Actions: nudge customers to re-fund wallets (auto-recharge, [Managing customer finances](/guides/managing-customer-finances)), or diagnose why they paused — caps hit, too expensive, churned.

**Pattern: Values falling against the previous period**
- The market is cooling here. Budget attention elsewhere.

## What happens next

With a regular rhythm of reading location analytics:

- **Weekly scan** — sort Counties by Active, scan the Received column, flag any wide gaps for the supply team.
- **Monthly CSV exports** — feed into a P&L-by-geography dashboard so finance and ops share the same map.
- **Quarterly territory review** — compare this quarter's top-demand counties to last quarter's. Customer behavior shifts; your sourcing should track it.
- **Ad-hoc checks** — whenever a customer asks "why am I not getting leads in X?", open X's location page to see exactly how many bids compete there and what top-bid you're up against.

Location analytics is a reference surface, not a dashboard you stare at all day. Build the habit of opening it with a specific question, and it'll pay for itself every time you spot a gap before the customer does.
