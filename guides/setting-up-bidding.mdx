---
title: Setting Up Bidding
description: Turn on bidding for your tenant, configure scope/pricing per lead type, and gate customer access with the tenant-side switches that make customer bids work.
---

Before a customer can place a bid, you (the tenant admin) need to decide who can bid, open specific scopes and pricing rules on each lead type, and decide which cap/budget controls customers should see. This guide walks through those switches end-to-end.

For how bidding actually resolves auctions (smart pricing, positions, status rollups), see the [Bidding feature page](/features/bidding). For the customer-side UI, see the [Bids page](/customer-portal/bids) in the customer portal reference.

## Prerequisites

- Admin role access to your tenant.
- At least one [lead type](/features/lead-types) configured. Bidding rules are per-lead-type, so you can't open auctions without one.
- A sense of pricing. What's the minimum you'd accept for a lead? The maximum? What's a reasonable step increment?

## 1. Open the Bidding settings page

Click the settings gear in the rail on the far left, then choose **Bidding** under **Application**. The page groups four sections (**Global Bid Settings**, **Customer Bidding Access**, **Lead Cap Settings**, and **Budget Settings**). Change whatever you need across all four, then click **Save changes** on the floating save bar that appears at the bottom.

## 2. Configure global bid settings

The first section, **Global Bid Settings**, controls whether customers can bid at all.

| Field | What it does |
|-------|--------------|
| **Disable all customer bidding** | Kill switch. When enabled, no customer can place or edit bids in the customer portal. You can still manage bids yourself from each customer's **Bids** tab. |

Leave it off for normal operation. Flip it on when you're migrating platforms, pausing operations, or running a fully tenant-managed pricing model.

## 3. Configure customer bidding access

The **Customer Bidding Access** section decides who can use bidding, and what everyone else sees.

| Field | What it does |
|-------|--------------|
| **Minimum deposit for auto-access** | The deposit threshold. Once a customer's deposits hit this amount, they automatically get bidding access. Leave blank to require you to grant access manually. |
| **Modal Subject** | Heading on the access-restricted modal a customer sees before they've earned access. |
| **Modal Body** | Longer explanation on the same modal. Good place to say "Schedule an onboarding call first" or link to your requirements. |
| **Button Text** | Label on the call-to-action button in the modal. |
| **Button Link URL** | URL the button opens. Common choices are a Calendly link, your sales form, or your Terms page. |

<Tip>
To grant or revoke access one customer at a time, use the **Can Access Bidding** toggle when you [edit the customer](/features/customers#creating-and-editing-a-customer). The threshold plus modal is the right tool when you have a consistent policy (e.g., "must deposit &#36;500 before bidding").
</Tip>

## 4. Decide which lead caps are available

Scroll to **Lead Cap Settings**. Each switch controls whether that cap type is available as a form field. Turning one off hides the field, and limits of that kind stop being enforced, even if customers had values saved.

| Switch | Scope |
|--------|-------|
| **Bid Daily / Weekly / Monthly Lead Caps** | Per-bid caps on number of leads won (resets each period). |
| **Account Daily / Weekly / Monthly Lead Caps** | Customer-level caps applied across all bids. |

The usual pattern is to enable monthly and daily (weekly rarely matters), but this is a product decision, so enable whatever your customers will actually use.

## 5. Decide which budgets are available

**Budget Settings** mirrors the cap section, but for dollar amounts instead of lead counts.

| Switch | Scope |
|--------|-------|
| **Bid Daily / Weekly / Monthly Budgets** | Per-bid dollar caps (resets each period). |
| **Account Daily / Weekly / Monthly Budgets** | Customer-level dollar caps across all bids. |

Budgets and caps are independent, so a bid stops on whichever hits first. See [Budgets and caps](/features/budgets-and-caps) for the full interaction model.

Click **Save changes** on the save bar when you're done with Steps 2–5.

## 6. Enable scopes on each lead type

Tenant settings are done; now move to the per-lead-type bidding configuration. Open **Lead Types** under **Ingest** in the sidebar, click a lead type, then click the **Bidding** tab.

The tab shows a **Bidding Scopes** table of the scopes that currently exist on this lead type. If there are none, customers can't bid on it yet. Click **New bidding scope** in the top-right.

  <img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/tenant/ingest/lead-type-bidding-dark.webp" alt="Lead type Bidding tab with county, state, and nationwide scope rows and a New bidding scope button" />
</Frame>

The dialog has these fields.

| Field | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| **Scope** | Required. Pick **County**, **State**, or **Nationwide**. Each scope is a separate row, so a lead type can support all three by creating three entries. |
| **Enabled** | Toggle. Leave on unless you want to prep a scope but not open it yet. |
| **Fixed Price** | Toggle. If on, every bid on this scope is at one set price, and the form collapses to a single **Price** field. If off (the default), customers bid within a range set by minimum, maximum, and step. |
| **Minimum** / **Price** | Required. The floor bid (or the only price if fixed). |
| **Maximum** | Required for range pricing. The ceiling bid; must be at least the minimum. Hidden when Fixed Price is on. |
| **Step** | Required for range pricing. The increment between valid bid amounts. For example, step `5.00` means customers can bid `$10, $15, $20…` but not `$12`. It can't be more than the maximum. Hidden when Fixed Price is on. |

Click **Create**. The scope appears in the table. Repeat for each scope you want to open (county, state, nationwide).

<Note>
A scope row is what makes that scope available to customers. If your lead type only has a **County** row, customers can only place county-level bids, and state and nationwide locations won't offer this lead type at all.
</Note>

## 7. Set per-customer limits and bids (optional)

If a specific customer needs a tighter cap or budget, set it on the customer rather than the tenant.

- For **account-level limits**, open **Customers**, pick the customer, and click **Edit**. The **Bidding Limits** section shows whichever account caps and budgets you enabled in Steps 4 and 5.
- For **bid-level limits**, open the customer's **Bids** tab and edit a bid. The **Bidding limits** section on the bid form shows whichever bid caps and budgets you enabled.

The **Bids** tab is also where you can create, edit, pause, or delete bids on the customer's behalf (useful when **Disable all customer bidding** is on, or for hands-on account management). Click **New bid**, search for a location, pick a lead type, and choose an amount. The tenant form also lets you go below the minimum or enter a custom amount.

## 8. Verify a customer can bid

Log in as a test customer. The customer's sidebar should show **Bids**. Clicking it opens the bid table; clicking **New bid** opens the bid form.

Check that the bid form shows these things.

- The lead types you expect for a location
- The price options you configured
- Cap/budget fields matching what you enabled

If it does, you're good. If something's missing, double-check the lead type's **Bidding** tab, since most "I can't see my lead type" issues trace back to a missing or disabled scope row there.

## What happens next

Once bidding is open, here's what happens.

- **Customers place bids** through the customer portal. They pick a location, a lead type, and an amount within your min/max/step rules. See the [Bids page](/customer-portal/bids) for the full customer walkthrough.
- **Auctions resolve in real-time** when matching leads arrive. The highest eligible bid wins, with smart pricing applied so the winner only pays one increment above the runner-up.
- **Statuses cascade**. Bids reflect their own health (<span className="tone-badge tone-success">Active</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-warning">Paused</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-sky">Limited</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-neutral">Archived</span>), so customers can debug why they're not winning by reading the status column.
- **Distribution stages** control *how* bids compete with other distribution methods. Make sure your **Highest Price** stage has **Enable Bids** turned on, otherwise your carefully configured bids never enter the auction. See [Distribution](/features/distribution).

Here's the full pipeline. You decide who can bid → you configure scopes and pricing → customer funds their wallet ([Managing customer finances](/guides/managing-customer-finances)) → customer places bids → leads flow in and auctions resolve. From here, natural next steps are adding [partners](/features/partners) for non-auction fallbacks, or [Configuring distribution stages](/guides/configuring-distribution-stages) if you want a multi-stage pipeline.
