Bidding is one of the primary ways leads get assigned to customers. When a lead comes in, Juiced runs a real-time auction. Customers with active bids compete, and the highest bidder wins the lead. It's like eBay, except the auctions happen in milliseconds and nobody's bidding on vintage lunch boxes.

## How bidding works

A bid is a customer's standing offer to purchase leads matching specific criteria. When a lead arrives that matches a bid's lead type and location, that bid enters the auction. The outcome depends on several factors.

- Higher **bid amounts** win.
- The **customer wallet** must have sufficient funds.
- The **customer status** must be active (not paused or archived).
- The **bid status** must be active (not paused or archived).
- **Caps and budgets** must not be exhausted.
- **Competition** decides the rest, meaning how this bid compares to others on the same lead type and location.

### The lead type connection

Every bid is placed on a **lead type**. The lead type's bidding settings determine how customers can bid.

| Setting | What it decides |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Which scopes are available** | Can customers bid at the county, state, or nationwide level? |
| **Fixed price vs. range** | Is there a single set price, or can customers bid within a range? |
| **Minimum bid** | The floor price to participate (or the only price when fixed pricing is enabled) |
| **Maximum bid** | The ceiling (hidden when fixed pricing is enabled) |
| **Bid increments** | The step between valid bid amounts (hidden when fixed pricing is enabled) |

You set these per lead type on its **Bidding** tab (open **Lead Types** under **Ingest**, pick a lead type, then **Bidding**). Each scope you open gets its own row with **Enabled**, **Fixed**, **Minimum**, **Maximum**, and **Step** values. Customers can only bid on scopes that have an enabled row.

### Scopes

The scope defines the geographic reach of a bid.

| Scope | What it covers | Example |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| **County** | A single county | "Los Angeles County, CA" |
| **State** | An entire state | "California" |
| **Nationwide** | The whole country | "United States" |

Broader scopes typically mean more lead volume but also more competition. A nationwide bid competes with every other nationwide bid on that lead type, plus all the state and county bids for the lead's specific location.

## Creating a bid

Customers place their own bids from the [Bids page](/customer-portal/bids) in their portal. You can also place, edit, and manage bids for them. Open **Customers**, pick a customer, then open the **Bids** tab and click **New bid**.

The bid form asks for three things.

1. Pick a **Location** by searching counties, states, and countries. The bid covers the area you pick, so the location sets the scope.
2. Pick a **Lead type**. Only lead types that accept bids for that location are listed.
3. Pick an amount for **My bid**. The form shows the current **Top bid** for that lead type and location so you know what you're up against.

The amounts offered follow the lead type's minimum, maximum, and step. Because you're the one running the stand, the tenant form also offers amounts **below minimum** and a **Custom** amount, which the customer's own form doesn't.

### Optional limits

If you've enabled bid-level limits in your [bidding settings](#bidding-settings), the form also has a **Bidding limits** section.

- **Daily/weekly/monthly lead caps** set the maximum number of leads this bid can win.
- **Daily/weekly/monthly budgets** set the maximum spend for this bid.

These are separate from customer-level caps and budgets. A bid might hit its own limit even if the customer still has capacity. See [budgets and caps](/features/budgets-and-caps).

## Managing bids

The customer's **Bids** tab lists every bid with its location, **Status**, **My Bid**, **Top Bid**, **Position**, and **Lead Type**, plus **Caps** and **Budgets** columns when those limits are enabled.

  <img className="hidden dark:block" src="/images/tenant/customers/customer-bids-dark.webp" alt="A customer's Bids tab listing bids by location with Status, My Bid, Top Bid, Position, Lead Type, Caps, and Budgets columns and a New bid button" />

Search by location, or use **Filters** to narrow by lead type or scope, or to include archived bids. Each row has these actions.

- **Edit** changes the amount or limits.
- **Pause / Unpause** turns the bid off or on without deleting it.
- **History** shows every change to the bid over time.
- **Delete** removes the bid (it's archived, and you can still find it with the archived filter).

To see every bid on a lead type across all customers, open the lead type's **Overview**. Its **Bids** table lists customer, location, status, and amount, with filters and an **Export** button.

<Note>
Pausing a bid is different from pausing a customer. A paused bid shows as "Paused" in its own status, but if the *customer* is paused, all their bids are functionally paused even though each bid might still display as "Active."
</Note>

## Bid statuses

Like customers, bids have multiple underlying statuses that roll up into a single **display status**. The display status is what you'll see most often. The underlying statuses appear as columns in the bids table on each location's page under **Locations** (see [location analytics](/features/location-analytics)).

### Underlying statuses

These four statuses each track a specific aspect of the bid's health.

| Status | What it tracks | Possible values |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Operational** | Whether the bid is manually on or off | <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Active</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-warning">Paused</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-neutral">Archived</span> |
| **Funding** | Customer wallet relative to bid amount | <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Available</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-warning">Limited</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> |
| **Cap** | Lead cap consumption | <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Unlimited</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Available</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> |
| **Budget** | Budget consumption | <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Unlimited</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Available</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-warning">Limited</span> <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> |

### Display status

The display status combines the underlying statuses into a single, summarized value.

| Display status | What it means |
|----------------|---------------|
| <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Active</span> | Everything is working. The bid is on, the wallet covers the full bid amount, and every cap and budget has room. |
| <span className="tone-badge tone-warning">Paused</span> | The bid itself has been paused. |
| <span className="tone-badge tone-sky">Limited</span> | The bid is still competing, but at a reduced amount. Either the wallet covers the minimum bid but not the full amount, or the budget left for the period is smaller than the bid. |
| <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> | The bid isn't receiving leads. The wallet can't cover the minimum bid, a lead cap has been reached, or a budget has been used up for the period. |
| <span className="tone-badge tone-neutral">Archived</span> | The bid has been archived or deleted. |

### How statuses resolve to display

The display status is derived from the four underlying statuses, checked in order. The first one that applies wins.

1. An archived bid is <span className="tone-badge tone-neutral">Archived</span>.
2. A paused bid is <span className="tone-badge tone-warning">Paused</span>.
3. If funding, the lead cap, or the budget is exhausted, the bid is <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span>.
4. If funding or the budget is limited, the bid is <span className="tone-badge tone-sky">Limited</span>.
5. Otherwise it's <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Active</span>.

A bid automatically returns to <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Active</span> when the constraint resets (a new day, week, or month) or the customer adds funds.

## Bid positions

Position tells you where your bid stands relative to the competition for the same lead type and location. It's your quick answer to "Am I winning?"

| Position | What it means |
|----------|---------------|
| <span className="tone-badge tone-success">Top</span> | You have the highest bid. Leads matching this lead type and location come to you first. |
| <span className="tone-badge tone-warning">Tied</span> | You're tied for the highest bid with one or more other customers. Leads are distributed round-robin among tied bidders. |
| <span className="tone-badge tone-caution">Under</span> | Someone else is bidding higher. You'll only get leads if higher bidders hit their caps or run out of funds. |
| <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Out</span> | Your bid isn't being considered at all. This happens when the bid is paused, archived, or the customer's funding is exhausted. |

<Tip>
If you're "Under," you can still win leads. You're just not first in line. Higher bidders might hit their caps or budgets, at which point leads cascade down to lower bidders.
</Tip>

## Bidding limits

Bids can have their own caps and budgets, separate from the customer's account-level limits.

### Lead caps

Lead caps limit the **number of leads** a bid can win.

- A **daily cap** resets each day.
- A **weekly cap** resets each week.
- A **monthly cap** resets each month.

When a cap is reached, the bid's display status shows as <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> (and its underlying **Cap** status reads <span className="tone-badge tone-danger">Exhausted</span> too) until the period resets.

Limits reset on the calendar day, week, or month in **UTC timezone**, not your local time.

<Note>
Lead caps only count leads won through bidding. If a customer purchases leads from the marketplace, those don't count against bid caps. That's why these are called "bidding limits," because they specifically govern the bidding channel.
</Note>

### Budgets

Budgets limit the **amount spent** through a bid.

- A **daily budget** sets the maximum daily spend.
- A **weekly budget** sets the maximum weekly spend.
- A **monthly budget** sets the maximum monthly spend.

When a budget is consumed, the bid transitions from "Limited" (can still participate at reduced capacity) to "Exhausted" (completely out of the auction).

### How limits interact

A bid respects the most restrictive constraint. Say you have these limits.

- A daily cap of 10 leads
- A daily budget of &#36;500
- A bid amount of &#36;40

You'll stop receiving leads when you hit 10 leads *or* &#36;500 spent, whichever comes first. In this example, 10 leads at &#36;40 each would cost &#36;400, so you'd hit the cap before the budget.

The same logic applies at weekly and monthly levels, and also considers customer-level limits. The bid stops when *any* relevant limit is reached.

## Bidding settings

Beyond individual bids, there are tenant-level settings that control how bidding works across your operation.

Open them from the settings gear in the rail, under **Settings → Application → Bidding**. The page has four sections (**Global Bid Settings**, **Customer Bidding Access**, **Lead Cap Settings**, and **Budget Settings**) and saves with the floating save bar.

### Disable all customer bidding

Under **Global Bid Settings**, **Disable all customer bidding** stops every customer from placing or editing bids in the customer portal. You can still manage bids from each customer's **Bids** tab.

This allows tenants to create a fully managed pricing system where they control the price of all leads.

### Customer bidding access

You can restrict customers from accessing bidding until they've deposited a minimum amount of funds. Set **Minimum deposit for auto-access** under **Customer Bidding Access**; once a customer reaches or surpasses the threshold, bidding access is automatically granted. Leave it blank to grant access by hand with each customer's **Can Access Bidding** toggle.

Tenants control the messaging shown to customers in the modal that blocks their access (**Modal Subject**, **Modal Body**, **Button Text**, and **Button Link URL**). This is useful for scenarios like requiring customers to schedule a call with your sales team before they can start bidding.

### Lead caps and budgets

**Lead Cap Settings** and **Budget Settings** decide which limits customers (and you) can set, at the bid level and the account level. See [budgets and caps](/features/budgets-and-caps).

## Smart pricing

Smart pricing ensures customers only pay what's necessary to win a lead, not their maximum bid. When a customer wins an auction, they pay one increment above the next highest bidder, regardless of how high they bid.

For example, if the minimum bid is &#36;10 with &#36;10 increments, and a customer bids &#36;100 while the next closest bidder is at &#36;10, the winner pays &#36;20, not &#36;100.

This encourages customers to bid their true maximum since they won't overpay when competition is low. It also means customers can confidently bid for top position without worrying about the actual price until the lead is won.
